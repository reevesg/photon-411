# Kite Peel (Asymmetric)

## Key Principles
- New kite hoists **inside** (between old kite and boat) on the second halyard and tack line
- Old kite must slide aft along the **outside** of the new kite when doused — keep it soft to minimize snagging
- **Do not touch the old kite until the new one is genuinely flying and taking load**
- Peeling up (new kite bigger) is easier — new kite blankets old well, old kite smaller and easier to gather
- Peeling down (new kite smaller) is harder — new kite may struggle to fill in the shadow of the old; be patient

---

## Prepare
- [ ] New kite pre-rigged in bag on deck — second halyard and second tack line attached
- [ ] New kite sheets led outside old kite sheets, clear of wrapping
- [ ] Squirrel below at companionway, ready on old kite lazy sheet
- [ ] Agree on timing and method with crew before executing

## Hoist New Kite
- [ ] Pit & mast: hoist new kite halyard — hand-over-hand fast
- [ ] New kite flies inside old kite
- [ ] Trim new kite sheet — wait for it to fill and take load
- [ ] **Confirm new kite is genuinely flying before proceeding**
- [ ] If peeling down: be patient — new kite may be slow to fill in the shadow of the larger old kite

## Douse Old Kite
- [ ] **Ease or blow old kite sheet** — let old kite go soft before releasing tack
- [ ] **Release old tack line** — old kite streams aft along outside of new kite
- [ ] Bowperson: guide old kite luff away from new kite as it slides aft
- [ ] **Drop old halyard fast** — squirrel pulls old kite in via lazy sheet through companionway
- [ ] Minimize time the two kites are in contact — fast drop reduces snagging risk

## After
- [ ] Old kite stowed below
- [ ] Old halyard and tack line secured and coiled
- [ ] Confirm new kite trimmed and flying cleanly
- [ ] Jib status — up or down as appropriate

---

> This maneuver requires practice to dial in — timing is everything. The checklist captures the sequence but expect to refine on the water.
