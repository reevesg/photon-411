# Tacking

## Roles
- **Skipper** — calls the tack
- **Main trimmer** — releases & resets traveler
- **Runner (port & stbd)** — one per side
- **Jib (port & stbd)** — one per side; windward releaser moves to grind for new sheet tailer

## Sequence

### Prepare
- [ ] Skipper: **"Ready to tack?"**
- [ ] Crew: **"Ready"**
- [ ] New windward runner crew: hands on runner, ready to pull
- [ ] New jib sheet tailer: sheet in hand, ready to pull
- [ ] Old jib sheet crew: ready to release, will then cross to grind

### Execute
- [ ] Skipper: **"Tacking / Hard alee"** — turn into wind
- [ ] Jib sheet crew: release old sheet as jib starts to luff
- [ ] New jib sheet crew: pull on new sheet quickly
- [ ] Old jib sheet crew: cross to new side and grind (primary winches not self-tailing)
- [ ] Feel the boat go flat as it passes through the wind
- [ ] New runner crew: pull on fully as boat goes flat
- [ ] Old runner crew: begin releasing once new runner is made — **do not release early**
- [ ] Main trimmer: release sheet and reset traveler to match position on new tack (conditions dependent)

### Trim
- [ ] Grind jib sheet to target trim
- [ ] Confirm both runners — windward set, leeward eased
- [ ] Main trimmer: final mainsheet & traveler trim
- [ ] Skipper: settle on new course

## Cross-Sheeting (Speed Tack)
Cross-sheeting pre-loads the new leeward sheet before the tack so the jib fills immediately on the new tack with minimal grinding.

- Before the tack, lead the new (lazy) jib sheet across the foredeck and back to the leeward winch
- Pre-tension so there is minimal slack when the jib crosses
- On the tack: release old sheet as normal — new sheet is already loaded and fills instantly
- Best used in light to moderate air where a fast fill is critical
- Less practical in heavy air or when foredeck is busy with kite gear
